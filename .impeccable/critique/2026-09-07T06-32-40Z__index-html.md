## What's Working

**The measurement strip is a component nobody can copy honestly.** `index.html:610-619` with `.strip` at 320-327: a flex-wrap container with `background:var(--line)` and `gap:1px` inside one `--line-strong` border, each cell `--surface` at min-width 118px, 17px IBM Plex Mono value over a 12.5px sans label. It works for three separate reasons — the value/label pair puts the number and its scope in one visual object so scope can never drift from figure; the gap-over-line trick gets internal dividers with one border and no per-cell rules; and it is the only element that spans the full content column, so it reads as the loudest thing in Selected Work without one point of extra type size or a drop of extra color. It is also the emotional peak of the page, which means craft and persuasion point the same way. Every reader who reaches it changes their mind about him. Every reader who doesn't, doesn't.

**Status honesty is built as a visual system, not a disclaimer.** Every record carries a dash-coded tick (solid = published/accepted, dotted = in prep) *before* it carries color, plus the written status, plus `author N / M` with his index bolded and his name marked in the author list (index.html:376-379, 390-396, 716-760). The legend is stated once. This is the opposite of the category norm, where early-career portfolios blur "in prep" into the same weight as "published". It survives desaturation and colorblindness because the line pattern carries the state, and it makes the one thing a PI is checking checkable in a single scan.

**The dragon is load-bearing, and the page is complete without him.** `body::after` puts a fixed 2px bar at `bottom:12px` (index.html:130-133) which is simultaneously the graticule's lowest division, the design's structural baseline, and exactly where `pet.js` computes `groundY` (`vh - 84 - 12`, GROUND_PAD frozen at 12). The graticule stops 12px short at `z-index:-1; pointer-events:none`; the footer holds 150px of bottom padding; nothing site-side is fixed at the viewport bottom; orange is spent nowhere else, so the dragon is always the highest-contrast event on screen. The design was built around him, not to make room for him afterwards. And `desktop-light-nopet-fullpage.png` proves the other half of the contract: with the pet off, nothing looks like it lost a limb. ### Charizard contract (6)

- [P2] **A containing-block property on `html` or `body` silently unfixes the dragon, and no document forbids it** — all three pet nodes are direct children of body (pet.js:567, 572, 580) and `groundY` derives from `window.innerHeight` alone (pet.js:111-115).
