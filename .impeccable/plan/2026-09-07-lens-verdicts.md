## charizard-contract

This build is a careful custodian of the dragon. The danger in the coming redesign is not that someone will break a clause that exists; it is that the two most likely ways to kill him are not clauses at all. First: a `transform`, `filter`, `backdrop-filter`, `contain`, or `will-change` on `<html>` or `<body>` makes body the containing block for all three injected nodes, un-fixes the pet from the viewport, and desynchronises his ground line from `body::after` — silently, visible only after a scroll. Second: the dark tail glow is a hardcoded `rgba(96,205,255,.55)` inside a frozen file, and it only reads as part of the system because dark `--sig` was chosen at rgb(95,208,230). Move the dark channel off cyan and you have a foreign blue halo behind the most-looked-at object on the page, with no legal way to fix it. Both need to become written clauses before anyone touches the palette or the page shell.

On the owner's tab idea: it is the right instinct and it is good for the dragon, with one hard collision to solve first. Good for him because his exposure over live evidence scales with scroll length — metrics.json records 7891px and 8.8 screens on desktop, 12040px and 14.3 on mobile, and three separate screenshots catch him standing on a publication title, on an `in prep` chip, and on two lines of a description. The collision is `:target`: it is a document singleton, the measurement cursor owns it (`.record li:target`, index.html:368-375), and a CSS-only `:target` tab switcher cannot coexist with it. A JS panel switcher can, but only if chip clicks reveal the destination panel *before* setting the hash — otherwise `:target` lands on a `display:none` row and the signature interaction silently does nothing. Decide that mechanism at layout time, not at build time.

The perch is more fragile than it looks and the toggle is less visible than it should be. The perch clamps rather than skips: `pos.x = clamp(textRight+28, 0, vw-96)`, so when the name outgrows its right margin the dragon does not stand elsewhere, he stands on the letters. Working the geometry, the pinch point is not 1440px (197px of slack, matching metrics heroH1.right=1119 exactly) but the 901px edge of the ≤900 breakpoint, where the gutter is still present and the name has only shrunk to 64.9px: 16.2px of slack. ## mobile-a11y

The desktop build is a genuinely well-made accessible document. The mobile build is not a build at all — it is the desktop document with the columns knocked out. Casey opens this at 390px, sees a header whose section nav is clipped mid-word at "Publi" with 286px of its 498px scrolled out of sight behind a hidden scrollbar, and then has exactly one affordance left: scroll 12,040px (14.3 screens; 16.1 at 360px). Selected Work alone is 4,682px — five and a half phone screens, 647 words — before the publication record even begins. PRODUCT.md defines mobile as "the follow-up glance." There is no glance available here. The theme rocker is the direct cause: it takes 78px of a 390px header for a control the phone's OS already sets, squeezing the nav to 212px. The nav is only complete above roughly 820px of viewport width, which means it clips on every phone, every portrait tablet, and every desktop at 175% zoom or higher — i.e. it fails precisely at the widths where the ability to skip a 14-screen scroll matters most.

On the accessibility side the fundamentals are right and should be said plainly: skip link first, real landmarks, a clean unskipped H1→H2→H3→H4 outline across all 30 headings, `.sr` "(opens in new tab)" on all 17 external links, status coded by dash pattern before color, `prefers-reduced-motion` honored by both the site and the pet, no horizontal overflow at 360px, and a focus ring that measures 5.13:1 (light) / 9.56:1 (dark) against every ground it lands on. All body and metadata colors clear AA: the worst text pair in the system is `--ink-faint` on `--surface-2` at 4.75:1, and `--sig` on `--surface-2` at 4.72:1. Nothing renders below 12px. That is better than most portfolios ship.

Three defects cut through that. First, the pet toggle's own boundary is 2.01:1 in light and 2.38:1 in dark at its resting `opacity:.62`, dropping to 1.51:1 / 1.67:1 with `filter:grayscale(1)` once it is switched off — a WCAG 1.4.11 failure, and worse, a one-way door: turn the dragon off and you cannot find the control that brings him back. Second, the site's third stated proof point — "author position is always shown" — is invisible to a screen reader at desktop widths, because `.pub-pos .pl{display:none}` strips the word "author" from the accessibility tree and the only remaining label is a `title` attribute on a non-interactive `<span>`, which is announced by nothing reliable. Sam hears "5 / 9". Third, the theme control is announced as a switch named "Dark theme" while being drawn as a two-cell Light | Dark segmented control with both visible labels `aria-hidden` — a voice-control user saying "click Light" gets nothing, and "click Dark theme" is a blind toggle, not a setter. The owner's tab idea is the right instinct for the mobile problem, but only if it ships with a real tablist and hash-synced state; otherwise it will break the chip→record measurement cursor and take the keyboard experience backwards.
